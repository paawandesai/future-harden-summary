# Member Messaging Guidebook

**Owner:** Coaching Content team. **Applies to:** every message that a member reads, including AI drafts that a coach approves.

Coaches approve each weekly summary before the member sees it. Coaches review dozens of drafts each morning, so they read fast. A draft that breaks a rule can reach a member if the coach misses it.

This guidebook has four rules. Each rule lists what a message must not do and gives examples. The examples do not cover every case. When a case is not clear, use the intent of the rule.

---

## Rule 1. No medical advice

Coaches are not clinicians. A message must not diagnose, name a likely condition, or recommend a treatment.

**Do not:**

- Name a cause or condition. Example: "That sounds like runner's knee."
- Recommend a treatment. This includes medication, supplements, ice, heat, braces, stretches for an injury, or a rest protocol.
- Tell the member to train through pain. Example: "Push through it" or "Work around the knee this week."

**When the member reports pain, injury, or illness this week:**

- Acknowledge it in one sentence.
- Say that the coach will contact them about it.
- Give no other advice about it.

**Allowed:** General recovery habits that are not about an injury. Example: "Sleep was strong this week. Keep that going."

## Rule 2. No body-shaming or weight pressure

A message must not treat body weight or body shape as a problem to fix.

**Do not:**

- Set a weight target or a weight deadline.
- Describe a weight gain as a setback. Example: "Let's get that number back down."
- Tie food, calories, or exercise to "earning" or "burning off" something.

**When `avoid_weight_talk` is true:**

- Do not mention body weight, body size, body shape, or appearance at all.
- This includes praise. "You look leaner" breaks this rule.
- The load on a lift is not body weight. "You added 10 lb to your squat" is allowed.

**Allowed:** Strength, energy, consistency, and performance. Example: "You added 10 lb to your squat."

## Rule 3. Brand voice

A message sounds like the member's coach. It is warm, direct, and specific to this member's week.

**Do:**

- Start with the member's first name.
- End with the coach's first name.
- Use 150 words or fewer.
- Mention at least one specific thing that the member did this week.

**Do not:**

- Make the member feel guilty about missed workouts. Example: "You skipped three sessions. No excuses next week."
- Use hype words. Examples: crushing it, beast mode, insane, unstoppable.
- Use more than one emoji.
- Sound like a bot. Examples: "As an AI", "Here is your summary:", or headings and bullet lists.

## Rule 4. Health data privacy

Members read summaries on their lock screen and in email. Other people can see these places. The clinical team also reviews lab results with each member in a separate visit.

**Do not:**

- Mention lab results. This includes the name of a test, a value, or a word such as "high" or "normal" about a result.
- Quote a biomarker value. Biomarkers are resting heart rate, HRV, and body weight.

**Allowed:**

- Trend words about biomarkers, with no number. Example: "Your recovery numbers improved this week."
- Activity data with numbers. Activity data is sleep hours, step counts, and workout details.

Rule 2 still applies to body weight. Use a trend word about weight only when Rule 2 permits a mention of weight.
